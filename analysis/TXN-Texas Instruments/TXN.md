## CAPEX Increase 2022-2025
Between 2022 and 2025, TI’s annual CAPEX surged from historical levels (around $1B–$2B) to roughly $5 billion per year. 
This shift is driven by three primary pillars:

**First: The 300mm Wafer Advantage** 

The core of TI's investment is the construction of massive 300mm (12-inch) wafer fabrication plants ("fabs"). 
- Cost Efficiency: A 300mm wafer yields about 2.3 times more chips than the older 200mm standard. This reduces the manufacturing cost of an unpackaged chip by approximately 40%. 
- The "Mega-Sites": Major funds have been directed to the Sherman, Texas site (planned for four fabs) and the Lehi, Utah site (LFAB). This allows TI to control its entire supply chain rather than relying on external foundries like TSMC.

**Second Geopolitical Resilience and "Onshoring"**

TI is aggressively shifting its manufacturing footprint to the United States to provide "geopolitically dependable" capacity.
- Goal 2030: TI aims to produce 95% of its wafers internally by 2030.
- Supply Security: By building in North America, TI is positioning itself as the most reliable supplier for industrial and automotive customers who want to avoid the risks associated with Asian-centric supply chains and potential trade disruptions.
- CHIPS Act Incentives: The timing of this CAPEX spike coincided with the U.S. CHIPS and Science Act, which provides TI with significant tax credits (a 25%–35% investment tax credit) and federal grants to offset these construction costs.

**Third: Targeting Secular Growth Markets**

TI is over-weighting its investments toward the Industrial and Automotive sectors, which now account for about 70%–75% of its revenue.
- Content Growth: Modern electric vehicles (EVs) and automated factories require significantly more analog and embedded chips than older models. TI is building capacity "ahead of demand" to ensure they don't lose market share during the next industry upcycle.
- Direct Sales Model: Parallel to factory spending, TI has invested in its TI.com infrastructure to move away from distributors. In 2024, approximately 80% of revenue was transacted directly, up from just 33% in 2019.
